Ans : "Yes but only with conditions"
The product is profitable on paper, but the margin is thin.

Critical points : 

1. Product margin shrinks fast. It drops from 26.6% to 11.6% once real costs are included. Only about 12 cents of every dollar is kept.
2. One refund, one return shipping bill or a slightly higher ad cost could wipe out the profit from several sales.
3. Pricing power is limited. The competitor is already at $149.95, so we can’t raise the price, and undercutting them would shrink the margin further.
4. As given cost ($110). Net profit - **$17.45**, Net margin - **11.6%**. 
5. "If cost is negotiated down to $100". Net profit - **$27.45**, Net margin - **18.3%**.

**So we can’t sell below about $132 without losing money.**

 Recommendations : 

1. Negotiate a better cost price or volume discount.
2. Check return rates and warranty for this product type.
3. Test with a small batch before a large order.
4. Consider bundling or an add-on to lift the order value.
 

My thought process : 

1. If I only look at the selling price and the product cost, it looks decent. We sell at $149.95 and buy at $110, so the gross profit is $39.95 per unit. Divided by the selling price, that’s a gross margin of about 26.6%. On its own, that looks quite healthy.

2. But gross profit isn’t what we actually keep, so I went through the costs one by one. The payment fee is 3% of the selling price, which comes to about $4.50. Add $8 for shipping and $10 for marketing, and we’re spending roughly $22.50 on top of the product cost.
Taking that from the $39.95, we’re left with about $17.45 profit per order. That’s a net margin of around 11.6%. So the margin more than halves once the real costs are included. We keep about 12 cents of every dollar of sales.

3. That’s still a profit, but it’s a thin one. If a single customer returns the product, or our ad cost creeps up even a little, we could lose the profit from several other sales. And we don’t have much room to move on price, because the competitor is already at $149.95. If we undercut them to win customers, the margin shrinks even further. I also worked out the break-even price, which is about $132. Below that, we lose money on every sale.

**Final Verdict and My recommendations:** 
 So my answer is yes, I’d sell it, but with conditions, not blindly. First, I’d try to negotiate the cost price. For example, at $100 instead of $110, our profit jumps to about $27 per order, which is a much safer cushion. Second, I’d check the return rate and warranty risk for this type of product. Third, I’d start with a small test batch to see real marketing and shipping costs before committing to a big order.
