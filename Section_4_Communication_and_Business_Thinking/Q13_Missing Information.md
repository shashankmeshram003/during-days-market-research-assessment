**Missing Information: Top 20 Best-Selling Products in Australia**

**My core approach**
Because no single source gives an accurate list, I would build the list by combining
several imperfect sources and being transparent about reliability.

1. **Define "best-selling"**: I would ask my manager whether they mean units, revenue, or
   popularity, and whether the scope is all products or During Days' categories. My
   working definition is products with the highest sales momentum in Australia over the
   last 90 days, measured through marketplace rankings, review growth and search interest.

2. **Sources**: Amazon Australia and eBay Australia best-seller/sold data; retailer
   best-seller pages (Kmart, Big W, Bunnings, Temple & Webster, Kogan); Google Trends
   (Australia); PriceSpy; Australia Post and ABS reports for category context; and
   community discussions.

3. **Validation**: A product must appear in at least 2-3 independent sources; I would remove
   sponsored listings, use only recent data, check seasonality, merge duplicate listings
   and flag anything I cannot verify.

4. **Ranking**: A simple points system (top-10 marketplace rank = 3 points, top-50 = 2,
   rising search interest = +1, strong review growth = +1), with number of sources as the
   tiebreaker. This point system (3 being highest to 1 being lowest)  will make the decision more smooth and easy to catch on.

5. **Presentation**: A ranked table with product, category, sources confirming, score,
   confidence level and average price, plus a method note and clear limitations saying -(this is
   an estimate from public proxies, not real sales data).

   **Example**: If a storage cabinet is #4 on Amazon AU Best Sellers (3 points), #12 on Kmart's best sellers (2 points) and trending up on Google Trends (+1), it scores 6 and is rated High confidence because it appears in 3 sources.
