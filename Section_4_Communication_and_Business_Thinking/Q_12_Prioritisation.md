**How would you prioritise these tasks?**
**You have 5 tasks and 4 hours**

 **SORT BY URGENCY AND IMPORTANCE**


1. Complete today’s competitor price report :  the deadline is Today, the urgency is high,the importance is high and the size is medium.

2.  Prepare tomorrow’s management report : the deadline is Tomorrow, the urgency is high,the importance is high and the size is medium.

3.  Fix 50 incorrect product records : the deadline is Not stated, the urgency is Medium,the importance is High (data quality) and the size is Small-medium.

4. Research a new product category : the deadline is Not stated, the urgency is Low,the importance is Medium (strategic) and the size is Large.

5. Find 100 new products : the deadline is Not stated, the urgency is Low,the importance is Medium  and the size is Largest.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------


**The priority order**

1. Today’s competitor price report : Time required is 60 minutes 
Why this at task at first? - It’s due today, and prices change daily, so the data loses value if delayed. It’s also the input for task 2.

2. Tomorrow’s management report : Time required is 60 minutes 
Why this at task at second? - It has a firm deadline and is visible to leadership. Doing it right after task 1 means I can reuse the fresh price data.

3. Fix 50 incorrect records : Time required is 45 minutes 
Why this at task at third? - Wrong data weakens every report and decision. It’s small and finishable, so it’s a good quick win.

4. Research a new product category : Time required is 40 minutes 
Why this at task at fourth? - Strategic but not urgent. I’d do the scoping and first-pass research, not the full job.

5. Prepare tomorrow's management report. : Time required is 10 minutes 
Why this at task at last? - Time for review and unexpected issues.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

**The reasoning**

1. **Deadlines come first**. Anything due today or tomorrow outranks open-ended work. The price report and management report are the only tasks with fixed dates, so they go first.

2. **Dependencies decide the order within those two**. The management report will probably use the price report’s findings, so doing them back-to-back saves time and keeps the numbers consistent.

3. **Quick, high-value tasks come next**. Fixing 50 records is bounded and improves everything downstream, so it earns third place. One assumption to state: if the incorrect records feed the price report or management report, I’d fix those specific records first, because wrong data would make the reports wrong.

4. **Large, non-urgent tasks come last**. Researching a new category and finding 100 products are the biggest and have no deadline. They’re important for growth but can’t beat deadline work, and they’re easy to split up

5. **Not everything will be finished, and I’d say so**. Four hours can’t realistically cover all five tasks fully. I’d tell my manager early which items will be complete and which will be partial, and agree on new timelines for the rest.
